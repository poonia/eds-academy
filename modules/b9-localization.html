Locale folder strategy

A bank is about to build /ca_en, /ca_fr and /us_en, each a full copy. aem.live’s advice would save years of duplicated work.

aem.live recommends a language-first structure in two tiers: language first, then market or region, such as /en/, /en/us/, /es/mx/. Market-first structures usually duplicate content, which pollutes search indexes and multiplies the work.

Use fallbacks where sensible, so a company focused on English-speaking US can serve that from /. Sheets work the same way: most placeholders live in the language folder and a market folder overrides some. Where markets share little, such as under differing regulation, market-based structures can be right.

Translation workflows

Translation is where cost, quality and timing collide. EDS leaves the mechanics to the tools you already chose.

EDS supports localization through the translation tools of your content source. In Experience Workspace, Google Translate is ready by default, with connectors for Lionbridge, Smartling and Trados. AEM authors use AEM’s translation tools and language masters.

Language detection should be a fallback only. A visitor arriving from a search result or a campaign is already in the right language, and aem.live notes that some legislation restricts geofencing. Always let people choose.

Multi-site authoring

Several brands or regions want a shared platform, but their teams want autonomy. Be clear what is shared and what is not.

With repoless, many sites share one codebase. With AEM authoring, MSM can keep Live Copies in sync with central content; this requires repoless. On document-based projects, each site has its own content location.

Generate hreflang with a configured sitemap per language. Pages relate through a common path suffix: /welcome and /fr/welcome. Set html-lang through bulk metadata, but note aem.live’s point that Google ignores it, so hreflang in the sitemap is what counts.

Hands-on lab: design a localization plan

Goal

Plan a real or fictional site in three languages and two markets.

You need

A site map, and someone who knows your markets.

Time

About 60 minutes

  1. List languages and markets.
  2. Find what truly differs by market.
  3. Draw the folder tree, language first.
  4. Decide translation methods by content type.
  5. Plan placeholders and which keys need market overrides.
  6. Define a language switcher and how to remember the choice.
  7. Draft the sitemap configuration with hreflang.

Expected result

A one-page plan with a folder tree, translation matrix and sitemap draft.

How to verify

Each market folder contains only what differs from its language folder.

Stretch

Estimate content volume under market-first and language-first structures.

Enterprise lens

Operating model. Decide who owns source content, translations and local exceptions, and document the handoffs.

Geo-blocking and forced redirects may be restricted in some jurisdictions. Involve legal before you add them.

Set the page language correctly so screen readers pronounce it properly, and write the language switcher in each language.

Avoid blocking the first paint on a geo lookup. Let visitors choose, and remember the choice.
