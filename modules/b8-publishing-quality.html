Preview, review, publish

Most content errors are caught by the second pair of eyes. Preview is what makes that possible without risking the live site.

The standard flow is simple: edit, preview, send the aem.page URL to reviewers, then publish. Preview shows the page with the current code, and visitors cannot see changes until you publish.

Check shared resources too. Publishing a page does not publish a nav, a fragment or a metadata sheet it depends on.

SEO authoring

A page nobody finds might as well not exist. Much of search performance is in the author’s hands.

Write a unique title and description, and use real headings. EDS serves a page’s canonical content in its initial HTML, which is what search engines first read. Set robots to noindex for pages that should not be found.

Canonical URLs are generated from cdn.prod.host unless you set canonical. The default description is the first paragraph with at least ten words. Use title:suffix style properties to add consistent branding from bulk metadata.

Accessibility authoring

An inaccessible page excludes people and, for many Canadian organizations, raises AODA obligations. Authors control much of it.

Developers build accessible blocks, but authors supply the meaning. Use one h1 and keep heading levels in order. Write alt text that conveys what an image communicates. Make link text meaningful out of context, and do not rely on colour alone.

Test with a keyboard, and check that a table is really tabular data. WCAG sets the standard, and your legal team decides what applies to your organization.

Scheduled and approved publishing

A rate change must go live at exactly 9:00 on the first of the month, after sign-off. The tools depend on how you author.

With Experience Workspace, Schedule Publish queues a page for a future time once it has been enabled, and Request Publish sends approvers an email with a link to review. Request Publish needs onboarding.

Other setups can register with Adobe’s scheduling service to schedule snapshots and pages. AEM authors can use Manage Publication. Older projects may have a crontab sheet in .helix. Adobe now lists it as deprecated, so do not use it for new work.

Content QA checklist

A checklist turns good intentions into habits, and catches what tired people miss.

Use a short, consistent list before each publish. Cover structure, metadata, links, images, mobile rendering and approval. Keep it short enough that people actually use it.

Teams can tailor it by content type: a blog post needs less than a regulated product page. The signature interactive below works as a starting point.

Answer for a real page. The checker tells you what is left to do.

Hands-on lab: QA a page before it goes live

Goal

Find and fix issues on a real or sample page.

You need

A preview URL, a keyboard, and DevTools or an accessibility tool.

Time

About 50 minutes

  1. Open the preview URL on desktop and a phone.
  2. Run the checker above.
  3. Review the heading outline.
  4. Tab through the page and check that focus is visible.
  5. Check View Source for your title, description and canonical.
  6. Fix the problems in the source and Update.
  7. Publish, then confirm on production.

Expected result

A page that passes every item, with notes on what you fixed.

How to verify

The checker shows Ready to publish and the head tags are correct.

Stretch

Tailor the checklist for your organization’s content types.

  • Reviewed on a phone
  • Checked headings and alt text
  • Tabbed through the page
  • Confirmed head tags
  • Published and confirmed

Enterprise lens

Audit. Keep evidence of who approved regulated content and when. aem.live also has an audit log.

Add accessibility checks to the definition of done, not as a final step.

A very large image or an extra block at the top of the page can cost LCP. Review new heroes for speed.
