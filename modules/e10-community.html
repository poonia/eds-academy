Block Collection and open source

Most of what you will build is a variation on something already solved. Contributing back makes that true for others.

Adobe’s Block Collection contains content blocks that are used widely across real projects, and that are general enough to reuse without changing their content model. Their principles are: intuitive, usable with no dependencies, responsive, context-aware, localizable, fast, and SEO friendly and accessible. Its main value is the content structure. Expect to change its CSS and JavaScript for your project. Blocks that are not used enough may be removed.

A self-contained, application-like feature, such as search results or a calculator, is a widget and does not belong in the collection. Widgets live in /widgets.

To contribute to an Adobe open source repository, read CONTRIBUTING.md. It asks you to check for an existing issue and overlapping pull requests, fork, work on a branch, and open a pull request that explains the intent, the change and anything that breaks. Third-party contributions need a signed Adobe Contributor License Agreement. Talk to the maintainers first, particularly for larger or AI-assisted changes.

Signature interactive: contribution flow

Step through a contribution, then put a pull request in order.

Use the block on real pages, and confirm the content model is simple.

Remove client names, hard-coded text and dependencies.

Confirm that your employer allows it, and sign the CLA.

Discuss the need with maintainers, and look for overlapping work.

Make focused changes, and pass lint.

Describe intent, changes and breakage, with a test URL.

Take review well. Rejection is a normal part of open source.

Hands-on lab: prepare a contribution

Goal

Get a block or fix ready to propose.

You need

A GitHub account, and permission from your employer if relevant.

Time

About 90 minutes

  1. Pick a block, or a small fix.
  2. Compare it against the principles.
  3. Remove anything project-specific.
  4. Search issues and pull requests.
  5. Read CONTRIBUTING.md.
  6. Draft the issue or pull request text.
  7. Decide with your team if you will submit.

Expected result

A contribution ready for discussion.

How to verify

A colleague can understand the need and scope from your text.

Stretch

Submit it.

  • Principles checked
  • Client details removed
  • Rights confirmed
  • Draft written
