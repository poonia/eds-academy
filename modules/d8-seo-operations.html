robots.txt and sitemaps

One wrong line can hide a site from search, and it is easy to miss until traffic falls.

aem.page and aem.live always disallow crawling, and add X-Robots-Tag: noindex, nofollow. Once traffic comes through your production domain, AEM serves a default or custom robots.txt, and that header goes away. The default allows crawling and points to your sitemap.

Disallow does not remove a page from Google. It can still appear in results, and crawlers can no longer see any noindex on it. To remove a URL, allow crawling and set robots: noindex.

AEM makes sitemap.xml from published documents without any setup, and supports index-based and manual configurations. Each sitemap holds at most 50,000 URLs and 50 MB. Set cdn.prod.host so that sitemap URLs use your real domain.

Metadata at scale

A site with 10,000 pages cannot rely on people remembering to fill out every field.

A metadata sheet in the content root applies values by URL pattern. It is read top to bottom, and a site-wide /** entry must come before more specific entries. A page’s own metadata block wins over the sheet. Preview and publish the sheet for changes to apply.

As sites grow, a single sheet becomes hard to manage. Adobe suggests dividing indexes, sitemaps and metadata into logical sections at 50,000 pages or more. An audit should catch missing titles, duplicates and bad canonicals. Set canonical to "" to remove it for a path if needed.

Multi-language SEO

Without hreflang, your French page may show to English searchers, or compete with its own translation.

Use one folder per language or region, and put hreflang in the sitemap, where AEM can generate it. Each language has its own source, destination, hreflang and an alternate path pattern. Pages are linked when they share a matching path.

Use real translated metadata, and self-referencing canonicals per language. Do not canonicalize every translation to English. Keep URL patterns parallel where possible, so that alternates can be calculated.

Find and fix SEO operational problems on a test site.

You need

A test site with a query index.

Time

About 60 minutes

  1. Open robots.txt on the aem.live host and note it.
  2. Open sitemap.xml.
  3. Add a metadata sheet with a site-wide image.

  5. Publish the sheet.
  6. Confirm the tag in page source.
  7. Rebuild or check the sitemap.

Metadata and crawl rules you can explain.

A noindex page has the meta tag, and is not in the sitemap if your index excludes it.
